# Real vendor shortlist verification

A shortlist fails when a candidate is plausible but wrong on one hard condition, or when a price
or certificate comes from an old page. Screen first, verify second, write short.

## Steps

1. List the hard conditions as a numbered set before looking at any candidate: certification or
   registration, size or compatibility, price ceiling, region, delivery. Anything the user did not
   state as a limit is a preference, not a condition.
2. Screen each candidate against every condition and reject on the first failure, with the
   condition and the figure that failed (for example 82 mm against a 77 mm thread). Do not bend a
   condition to keep a candidate.
3. For the survivors, state where each condition is verified: the maker's or authority's own page
   (certificate registry, official product page, regulator database), with its link and the date
   checked. A reseller listing or a cached page is not verification.
4. Mark what is unverified: a price older than the user's decision window, a page without a date,
   a certificate not found in the registry. A stale price keeps the candidate in the list but
   flagged, and the brief asks the user to confirm the current price.
5. Never invent a link, certificate number or price. If no link was supplied and you cannot browse,
   write "link not supplied" in that cell and say the check is still to do.
6. Return three to five candidates at most. When the user asks for a short brief, keep the whole
   reply, tables included, under 120 words: one line of conditions, one line of rejections with
   the failing figure, one table row per survivor, one line of what to confirm. No background, no
   buying tips, no explanation of terms unless asked. Count the words before sending.

## Output

Hard conditions, a rejected list with reasons, a table (candidate, exists as a current product
yes or not checked, each condition with status, source link or "not supplied", verified date), and
a one-line list of what to confirm. Existence of the product is itself a check: say "not checked"
when you cannot open the maker's page, and never call a candidate confirmed on the user's notes.
End with one plain sentence naming what is unverified for each survivor, for example "D's
existence, current price, thread size and ND rating are not verified on the maker's page." The buyer
makes the choice.
