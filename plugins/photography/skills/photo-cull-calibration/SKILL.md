# Photo cull calibration

Photographers report that a generic person-centred cull misjudges wildlife, drops frames
that matter at an event, and picks a burst winner by rules that do not fit the subject. The
skill works only from what the user supplies (scores, flags, filenames, descriptions); it does
not look at pixels it was not given.

## Steps

1. Name the subject type and write 3 to 5 criteria for it before judging any frame. Examples:
   a bird in flight is judged on eye sharpness, wing position and head angle, and a closed
   nictitating membrane is normal bird anatomy, not a "closed eye" defect; an event is judged
   on the key moments list; a burst on focus, expression and composition within the group.
2. Calibrate: ask the photographer for 10 to 20 frames they already kept and rejected from this
   kind of shoot, and compare the criteria against them. State that criteria are provisional
   until that comparison is done and report any kept frame the criteria would have rejected.
3. Split every flag into two lists. Technical flaws (blur, missed focus, clipped highlights) are
   candidates to rank down. Moments that matter (a toast, a hand-off, a rare behaviour) are
   never rejected on a technical flaw alone and go to a must-review list.
4. Never delete or recommend deleting. Output a ranking and flags only, keep every original,
   and say the photographer reviews all frames, including those ranked lowest.
5. Reconcile counts: frames or groups supplied versus frames or groups scored. List each
   missing id, and say that a tool silently skipping frames is a reason not to trust its pick.

## Output

A table: frame id, rank, technical flags, moment-that-matters note, action (keep, review,
rank down). Then the criteria and their calibration status, the missing-frame list, and the
questions the photographer must answer before the ranking is used. State what could not be
verified because no pixels were supplied.
