# Manuscript continuity audit

A long manuscript outgrows anyone's memory, and an assistant that "reads the chapter" tends to
praise it or quietly fix it. The job here is narrower: find where the new text disagrees with
what the author already decided, show both sides, and let the author rule.

## Inputs

- The new text (chapter or scenes).
- The author's own sources: canon ledger or earlier chapters, the approved outline, character
  notes, and the chosen tense and point of view. Use only these. A fact that is not in them is
  not canon; say "not in the ledger" instead of guessing.
- If there is no ledger, build one first from the earlier text given: names, ages and dates,
  geography, rules of the world, and who knows what, each with the chapter where it is set. Show
  it and mark it as a draft the author confirms before relying on it.

## Steps

1. **Canon check.** Compare every fact in the new text with the ledger. For each conflict give
   the ledger entry and its chapter, the new line quoted, and what differs. Cover numbers
   (ages, dates), geography (direction, distance, who crosses what), rules (what is
   possible, what a place or object does), and who knows what at that moment.
2. **Outline check.** Compare each scene with its outline entry. List every difference as a
   drift: outline says, draft does. Do not call it an error. Mark each as "intended change?" or
   "accidental?" as a question for the author, since a good draft often improves on its outline.
3. **Scene purpose and repetition.** Per scene state the new information, the tension and what
   it contributes. When information was already given, name the earlier location.
4. **Motivation.** For each major action cite the earlier scene that motivates it, or write
   "no earlier motivation found". Do not invent a motive to close the gap.
5. **Tense and POV.** Report each shift by paragraph (or sentence when short): the established
   tense and POV, the line that departs from it, and a question - intended narrator change or
   slip? Do not rewrite the line.

## Rules

- Quote, do not paraphrase, both the canon source and the draft line. Locations are chapter and
  paragraph.
- Never rewrite, polish or "improve" the author's prose and never offer replacement wording
  unasked. If the author asks for options after the audit, give them then.
- Separate three kinds of finding in the output: contradictions with canon, drift from the
  outline, and craft observations (repetition, motivation, tense). Genre fit, taste and
  quality are the author's judgement and stay out.
- When a reported fact could be intentional (an unreliable narrator, a lie by a character,
  a revision of canon), say so in the same row instead of ruling it out.

## Output

A table per kind: location, canon or outline source (quoted), draft line (quoted), what differs,
and the question for the author. Then a short list of questions the author must answer to
update the ledger. If a kind has no findings, write "none found" for it, with what was checked.
