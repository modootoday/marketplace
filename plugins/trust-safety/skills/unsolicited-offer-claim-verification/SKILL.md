# Unsolicited offer claim verification

A model cannot prove a message safe or fraudulent from its text. What it can do is split the
message into claims and show how the person can test each one without trusting the sender.

## Steps

1. **List the claims.** One row per claim: the sender's stated identity and company, the
   channel it arrived on, the offer and pay or return, any fee or deposit asked, the deadline,
   and the action requested (click, reply, pay, share a document or code).
2. **Independent check per claim.** Say how to test it through a source the person finds
   themselves: the company's own website reached by typing its name or from a known source,
   a phone number from that site, the official job or registry listing, the platform's own
   inbox. Never use the link, number or reply address in the message. For a refund or prize,
   check through the account or organisation named, found independently.
3. **Risk signals.** Mark which features are commonly seen in scams, for example an upfront
   fee to start, pay far above the task, urgency, an unknown number, a request for codes or
   ID. Call them signals, not proof. A message with none can still be fraud, and a message
   with some may come from a careless real firm.
4. **Verdict wording.** Do not say it is definitely safe or definitely a scam, and do not
   accuse a named real company; say what the checks would show and what remains unverified.
5. **Next steps.** Advise no payment, no sharing of data, codes or documents, and no
   clicking until the checks are done. If money or data was already sent, point to the
   person's bank or payment provider and their national fraud-reporting service, found
   through an official government site, and keep the message and receipts.

## Output

A claims table (claim, independent check, signal), a short list of risk signals, what stays
unverified, and the do-not-pay line with the reporting step. State that this is a pre-check,
not a finding.
