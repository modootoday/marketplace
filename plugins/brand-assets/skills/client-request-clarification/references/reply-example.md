# Reply email shape: defaults, silence date, no delivery promise

Synthetic example, not the user's request. Client message: "Hi, could you do something for our bookshop's
spring reading night, like the one before but nicer? Logo attached. Soonish please. Thanks, Ravi."
The designer does not know the earlier piece, has no brand guide, and gave no price or delivery date.

## Four questions, each one with a one-word default

1. Format? Default: one A5 portrait poster, print and screen. Confirm with "yes".
2. Event facts? Default: the same wording as the earlier event notice with the new date; I will not
   invent any of it. Confirm with "yes".
3. Earlier piece? Default: if you cannot find it, I start fresh from the logo. Confirm with "yes".
4. Timing? Default: I treat the event day as the deadline and tell you my delivery day once your answers are in.
   Confirm with "yes".

Each default is a statement the client can accept whole. "Tell me the date" or "send the wording" asks for
more work and fails that test; the approver, language and revisions go to the assumptions list.

Anything else (language, revisions, "nicer" meaning) goes to the assumptions list, not to a fifth question.

## Draft email rules shown above

- The email states the date by which silence means the defaults stand, written as a relative or placeholder
  date the designer fills in ("two working days after you receive this", "[date]"). It is a reply-by date,
  not a delivery date.
- The email promises no delivery date, price, number of revisions or hours of work, because the designer
  supplied none. Where the client's own date is vague ("soonish"), the question asks for the date; the
  designer's date for final files is left as "[you decide]".
- Do not turn a vague word into a calendar date or a channel. "Soonish" stays a question; do not write
  "Friday" or "Instagram" as if known. A format or channel appears only inside a default the client can
  reject, and the draft says it is a proposal.
- A fact the email lacks (event details, the offer) is requested, never filled in.

## Checklist shape

Known facts (quote the email), assumptions (marked as such), files to collect (logo as vector, earlier piece,
exact copy), blocked until answers arrive (final copy, size), and what may start now (inspect the logo file).
