# Study calendar allocation

A book without pacing needs arithmetic before it needs a calendar. Show capacity against demand first,
then place the work. For one day's hour-by-hour plan use constrained-day-schedule; this skill spreads
work across weeks.

## Steps

1. Collect: units and hours per unit, review chapters or appendices and their hours, weeks left, days
   per week, hours per day, break weeks or holidays, each learner's extra needs, shared subjects,
   and the parent's availability. State every assumption you make for a missing number.
2. Compute capacity: usable weeks = weeks left minus break weeks; hours = usable weeks times days
   times hours per day. Show the arithmetic.
3. Compute demand: units times hours, plus each optional block listed separately. Show the total with
   and without optional blocks (review chapters, appendices) against capacity, as shortfall or slack in
   hours and in weeks.
4. Do not exclude optional blocks yourself. Ask the user, or show both plans and let them choose.
5. Place units week by week in a table (week, units, hours used, hours left). Keep slack as named
   buffer weeks, not spread thin.
6. For several children, put the shared subject in the same weekly slot for all of them, then add each
   child's extra hours in slots that do not overlap the parent's supervision of another child or
   the parent's unavailable times. Check each slot against the parent's times and list any clash.
   Read references/slot-grid-example.md before drawing the grid: parent-dependent slots (shared subject,
   a child's extra hours) go only on days the parent is free, rows for the parent's away days hold
   independent work only, and a "Checked:" line names each slot's parent day.
7. Show slippage for every plan you present, each with its own line in hours: hours lost when one
   week is missed (hours per week), the new capacity, and the new slack or shortfall against that
   plan's demand (for example "plan with reviews: 80 h against 72 h left, 8 h short"; "plan without:
   72 h against 72 h, slack used up, still just fits"). Then say which units move and what to cut
   first. A plan with no buffer still needs its number, not "no buffer".
8. This is a plan from the numbers given. The parent or learner decides pace and content; unit
   difficulty and the child's progress are not assessed.

## Output

Capacity and demand arithmetic, the shortfall or slack with and without optional blocks, the week table,
the weekly slot grid with the conflict check, the slippage scenario and the decisions left to the user.
