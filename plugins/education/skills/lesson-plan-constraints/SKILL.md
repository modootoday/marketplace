# Lesson plan constraints

A lesson plan fails quietly when its minutes do not add up or a support is promised but never placed.
Build the plan from the constraints and check each one in writing.

## Steps

1. List the constraints as given: period length, objectives, learner needs or supports, any fixed
   slots or caps, class size and room facts. Mark any that are missing and state the assumption.
   Use the needs as described; do not diagnose or label students.
2. Build a table of activities: start-end minute, minutes, activity, who is talking or working, the
   objective it serves (a number, or the word routine for attendance, transitions and other non-teaching
   time; no in-between labels such as prepares). Include opening, transitions and close; transitions take time.
3. Sum the minutes and show the sum next to the period length. If it is off, repair the plan until
   it is equal, and say what you changed.
4. Map objectives to activities both ways: every objective has at least one activity that lets the
   learner show it, and every activity serves an objective or is labelled as routine (attendance,
   transition). Delete or justify an activity that serves nothing.
5. For each stated support, name a concrete accommodation and the activity where it happens (for
   example "large-print worksheet in minutes 15-25", not "support for visual needs"). Add the
   materials it needs. Say if a support cannot be placed within the time.
6. Check caps and fixed slots against the table by adding the relevant minutes (for example teacher
   talk totals 11 minutes against a cap of 12).
7. List materials grouped by activity, with quantities when the class size is known. Mark anything the
   teacher must prepare in advance.
8. The teacher adapts the plan to the room. State what you could not know (prior knowledge, room
   layout, student relationships).

## Output

Constraints list with assumptions, the activity table with the minute sum, the objective mapping,
the accommodation table, the cap checks, the materials list and the unknowns line.
