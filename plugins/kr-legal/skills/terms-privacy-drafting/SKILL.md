# Drafting terms and a privacy policy

A policy that describes a product the company does not run is worse than none:
it promises things nobody does and omits what actually happens. Draft from the
facts, then from the law.

## Collect the facts first

Ask for, or read from the code and configuration:

- each category of personal data, where it comes from, why, and how long it is
  kept;
- every processor (hosting, email, payments, analytics, AI providers) and its
  country;
- payments: what is sold, how renewal, cancellation and refunds work;
- user content: who owns it, what the service may do with it, takedown process;
- age limits, account deletion, contact point for privacy requests.

Any fact you cannot confirm becomes a bracketed placeholder in the draft, not a
guess.

## Privacy policy

Cover the items the Personal Information Protection Act requires in a published
policy (purposes, items, retention, provision to third parties, entrustment and
processors, overseas transfer with its details, destruction, data subjects'
rights and how to exercise them, safety measures, the privacy officer). Retrieve
the current article list with the `korean-law` server and cite the version date.

## Terms of service

Service description, account rules, fees and refunds (in line with the
e-commerce and consumer rules that apply), user content and licences, prohibited
use, service changes and suspension, liability limits that the law allows,
dispute resolution and governing law. A clause that removes a consumer's
statutory right is void; do not write one.

## Output

The two drafts in Korean, a table of every placeholder still to fill, and the
list of articles relied on with their effective dates. Recommend counsel review
before publishing.
