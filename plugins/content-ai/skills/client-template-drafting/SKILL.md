# Client template drafting

Drafting from a client template fails in two quiet ways: the model fills a
gap with a plausible number, quote or date, and it forgets a decision the
client already made ("we never say 'solution'", "results are reported
against last year, not last month"). Both cost a round of client
corrections. This skill keeps the draft inside what the client and the user
have actually supplied.

This skill is a template. The company supplies the client pack; the skill
fills it and lists what is still missing.

## Inputs the company supplies

A client pack per client (template: `references/client-pack-template.md`):

- **Document template**: sections in order, each marked required or
  optional, with length and format limits.
- **Client rules**: terms, tone, banned and required phrasing.
- **Decisions log**: dated decisions from earlier rounds, each with who
  decided. Later entries win over earlier ones.
- **Facts for this document**: figures with their period and source, names
  and titles, dates, quotes approved by the person quoted.

Use one client pack. If facts or rules from another client are in the
conversation, do not use them.

## Procedure

1. Read the decisions log first and list the decisions that affect this
   document type. These override the template defaults where they conflict.
2. Map each supplied fact to a section. A fact without a source or period
   is used only with a placeholder asking for it.
3. Draft section by section in template order. Where a fact is missing,
   insert a visible placeholder: `[MISSING: what is needed, who can supply it]`.
   Never invent figures, quotes, customer names, dates or results.
4. Apply the client rules to the wording.
5. **Check** the draft:
   - every required section present and within its limits;
   - every decision from step 1 respected (list each with where it applies);
   - every number and quote traceable to a supplied fact;
   - no placeholder hidden in prose; all are in the bracket form.

## Stop and hand to a person when

- a required section cannot be written without facts nobody supplied;
- the decisions log conflicts with the template or with itself;
- the document makes a commitment (price, deadline, guarantee) not in the facts;
- a quote is attributed to someone without their approval on record.

## Output

1. The draft, with placeholders in place.
2. Unfilled placeholder list: section, what is needed, who can supply it.
3. Decisions applied: decision, date, where it shaped the draft.
4. Check results against the template's required sections.
