# Naver blog posts that get found

Naver ranks blog posts by two things it has described publicly: how much the
source has earned trust on a topic (C-Rank) and how useful and first-hand the
document is (D.I.A.). Neither rewards tricks. Most advice that circulates is
about tricks, so the job here is mostly to keep them out.

Korean examples for every step are in `references/post-checklist.ko.md`.

## Before writing: one search intent

Pick the one phrase a reader would type, and the question behind it. A post that
answers two unrelated questions ranks for neither. If the user brings several
topics, propose splitting them into separate posts.

## Title

- The search phrase appears once, early, in natural Korean word order.
- Say what the reader gets (a price, a route, a result), not a teaser.
- Keep it to one line on a phone; around 30 Korean characters is a safe target.
- No repeated keywords, no brackets full of keywords, no clickbait the body does
  not pay off.

## Body

- **Lead with the answer** in the first two or three sentences; the rest
  supports it.
- **First-hand detail** is the strongest signal: what the writer saw, paid,
  measured or tried, with dates. Rewritten text from other pages is the
  weakest, and copied text is demoted.
- **Subheadings every few paragraphs**, each one a question or a step, so a
  reader can scan.
- **The search phrase where it reads naturally**, a few times at most. Variants
  and related words do more than repetition.
- **Images the writer took**, each with a sentence of its own text; stock images
  and screenshots of other sites add nothing.
- **Disclose sponsorship** in the opening when there is any. Korean advertising
  rules require it, and readers trust the post more for it.

## Tags

A handful of tags that name what the post is actually about: the search phrase,
the place or product, the category. Tags unrelated to the post are spam.

## Review checklist

When reviewing a draft, go through the steps above in order and return each
finding with the line it concerns and a rewritten line. Do not promise a ranking;
say which signal each change strengthens.
