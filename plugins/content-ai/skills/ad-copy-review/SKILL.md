# Ad copy review

An ad fails in two ways: it says something the advertiser cannot prove, or it says
so much that the reader takes away nothing. Review for both, in that order.

## 1. Claims

List every factual claim in the copy, one per line, and mark each:

- **Shown**: the evidence exists and can be produced (a test report, a sales
  figure with its period, a certification number). Keep it, and note where the
  evidence lives.
- **Unshown**: plausible but not on hand. Ask for the evidence or soften to what
  is known ("used by 3,000 shops" rather than "the most popular").
- **Unprovable**: absolute or superlative words with no measurable basis - "best",
  "No. 1", "guaranteed", "100%", "the only", "perfect". Cut or replace them.

A comparison with a competitor needs the same test, the same conditions and a
named source; otherwise remove it. Never disparage another product.

## 2. Category rules

Some products carry their own limits. Flag, do not soften:

- **Food and supplements**: no claim to prevent or treat a disease, no wording that
  makes food read as medicine, and no health-function claim unless the product is
  registered as a health functional food.
- **Cosmetics**: no medical or drug-like effect ("cures acne", "regrows hair") and
  no functional claim (whitening, wrinkle, UV) unless the product is registered for it.
- **Medical devices, medicine, finance, loans**: stop and say these need a
  specialist or pre-review before running.

When the ad targets Korea, check against `references/rules.ko.md`.

## 3. Disclosure

Paid, gifted or affiliate content says so at the start, in words the reader sees
without expanding anything ("Ad", "Sponsored", "Gifted by ..."). A hashtag at the
end of a long caption is not enough.

## 4. One promise

The ad should leave one thing in the reader's head. Find the strongest true benefit,
put it first, and cut lines that compete with it. One call to action that says what
happens next ("Get the sample kit", not "Click here").

## Output

1. The claim table: claim, mark, evidence or action.
2. Category and disclosure findings, each with the rule it meets.
3. The revised copy with changes marked, and one line per change saying why.
4. What the user still needs to supply (evidence, registration numbers) before it runs.
