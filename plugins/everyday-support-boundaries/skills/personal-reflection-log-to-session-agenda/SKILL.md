# Personal reflection log to session agenda

The log is the user's. The reader who can interpret it is their clinician. This skill
turns entries into a tidy agenda and counts; it is not therapy and does not replace the
professional. It uses only the entries the user pasted and session notes they say they
have consent to use.

## Steps

1. Ask or confirm the indicators the user chose (for example the 1-5 score and the one-line
   note). If none were stated, ask before counting anything. Count only those.
2. List every entry with its date, score and the user's own words in quotes, unchanged.
   Do not paraphrase a note into a feeling or a cause.
3. Report plain counts and ranges for the chosen indicator: number of entries, lowest,
   highest, mean to two decimals, how many days at each score, dates with no entry. State
   that these are counts of what was logged, six days is too few to read a pattern, and
   that you do not judge them better or worse.
4. If the user asks "is this getting better?", answer that you cannot say that: it is for
   the therapist to interpret, and you report the counts only. Do not say improving,
   worsening, stable or a cause, even softly.
5. Draft questions for the session, each one citing the dated entry that raised it, phrased
   as the user's questions to ask ("On 10/2 I wrote 'argued with mom'; can we talk about
   that?"). Offer no answers, no theory, no technique.
6. If an entry describes danger to the user's life or another person, say so plainly, point
   to the contacts the user supplied and the local emergency number, and ask them to reach
   one now. Invent no hotline.
7. End with one line: the clinician interprets this log; this is not a diagnosis or a
   replacement for the session.

## Output

Sections: Indicators used; Entries (date, score, quote); Counts; Questions for the session
(each with its entry date); one closing line. Read `references/agenda-layout.md`.
