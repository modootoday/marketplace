# Item identification from photos

A photo supports candidates, not certainty. A listing that names the wrong model, or calls an
item genuine on a guess, misleads a buyer. This skill says what is seen, what it fits, and what
would settle it.

## Steps

1. Separate what is seen from what is assumed. List the visible facts only: item type, shape,
   colours, materials, logos, text, numbers and where each sits. Mark every marking as clear,
   partial or unreadable. Quote partial markings with the gaps shown, for example `XA_` for a
   cut-off character.
2. Give two or more candidates, most likely first, each as its own numbered entry. When a partial
   marking fits several variants of one family, list each variant as a separate candidate (for
   example the family name with each possible suffix) rather than one family entry; use a family
   entry only when the facts cannot separate variants. Each candidate has a name as specific as the
   facts support, the visible features that support it, and the feature that does not fit or is
   missing. Do not give one confident answer from a partial marking.
3. Ask for the photos that settle it, in order of how much each narrows the candidates: the model
   or type plate (often underside, back, inside a battery bay or on a sticker), the serial or
   batch number, the maker's mark on a flat clear surface, the lens or part ring text, and any
   included accessories. Say what each photo would tell you.
4. State plainly what photos cannot confirm: that the item is genuine rather than a copy or a
   re-badged unit, that it works, its condition beneath the surface, its completeness, its year,
   and its value. Do not call anything genuine, authentic, rare or vintage as a fact; the seller
   may describe it as "appears to be" a named candidate only after a marking is confirmed.
5. No price claims. Do not give a price, range, "worth", "sells for" or a comparison to sold
   listings; say price needs the confirmed model, condition and the seller's own research of
   sold listings. If asked for a price, decline that part and continue with the rest.
6. Offer neutral listing wording that states only what is seen and what is unconfirmed, for the
   seller to check against the object in hand.

## Output

Seen facts with marking clarity, ranked candidates with supporting and conflicting features,
the photos to request, the list of what cannot be confirmed, and the neutral wording.
