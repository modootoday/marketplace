# Interview briefing fact pack

A model preparing talking points for an interview invents a current statistic with a confident
source. The spokesperson then says it on the record. This pack lists only what the pasted
material supports and says plainly where it is silent.

## Steps

1. Record the inputs: the spokesperson and role, the outlet and format (live, recorded, print), the interview date and
   today's date, the approved key messages, the pasted sources, and any topics the organisation
   has said are off limits. If the interview date is unknown, state the staleness rule against today.
2. List the likely questions, one to two per theme, including at least two hostile or awkward ones that follow from
   the material (a recent complaint, a price rise, a delay). Do not invent events that the material
   does not mention.
3. For each question write: the approved key message (from the list, not new), the supporting figure,
   its source name, the source's date and a staleness flag. A figure is stale when its source date is
   more than 12 months before the interview; mark it "stale: confirm or drop" and give the age in
   months. A question for which no pasted source has a figure gets "no approved figure" and a line
   to ask the data owner. Never fill it with an estimate, a typical industry number or a figure from
   memory.
4. Add one to two bridge-back lines per theme, built only from approved messages, for answers that
   drift off topic.
5. Keep a separate off-limits list: topics the organisation barred, matters under legal or
   regulatory process, unannounced plans and other people's confidential information. For each,
   write a short neutral reply that declines without a denial, and mark it for the communications lead to approve.
6. List what to confirm before the interview: each flagged figure, the key messages' approver, and any question where the source material conflicts.

## Output

A table per theme (question, key message, figure, source and date, staleness flag), the
bridge-back lines, the off-limits list kept apart, and the confirm-before list. State that the pack
holds only pasted material, that no outside facts were added and that the spokesperson and the
communications lead decide what is said.
