# Stakeholder question rehearsal

Play a critical but fair reviewer of the plan. Evidence base: one report that a marketer
set the AI up as a critical director and found gaps in meeting preparation. Treat the method
as a rehearsal aid, not a prediction of what a real person will ask.

## Steps

1. Restate the goal and the plan in two lines each. If the goal or the decision being asked
   for is missing, say so and ask; every question below depends on it.
2. Write the hard questions, grouped by what they attack: the problem is not real or not
   big enough, the numbers, the timeline and cost, the risk, why us or why now, and what
   happens if we do nothing. Aim for the five to eight a sceptic would ask first, not a long list.
3. For each question attach the evidence the plan already contains (quote the bullet) or
   state "no evidence in the plan". Label each answer basis as DATA (a figure or source the
   plan gives), ASSUMPTION (stated or implied, not shown), or MISSING.
4. Flag logic gaps in the plan itself: a conclusion that does not follow from the bullets,
   a figure with no baseline, a benefit without an owner or date, a cost not mentioned, two
   bullets that conflict. Name the bullet.
5. Draft a short answer to each question, two sentences at most, using only what the plan
   supports: restate plan facts exactly (keep their period and units), never add a claim the
   plan does not make, such as a comparison done or a reason for a choice. Where the
   evidence is missing, the draft says what must be gathered, never an
   invented number or customer.
   Before sending, read `references/worked-example.md` and check each draft answer
   against its allowed-and-forbidden table; replace any sentence the plan does not support
   with a gather note.
6. Close with the three questions most likely to hurt and what to bring to the meeting.

## Output

A table: question, what it attacks, evidence in the plan, basis (DATA, ASSUMPTION, MISSING),
short answer. Then the logic-gap list and the three-question close. Do not state how an
actual named person will react.
