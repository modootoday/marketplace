# Decision memos

The reader has two minutes. The memo must let them decide in that time, and let
them see what they would be agreeing to.

## Shape (one page)

1. **Decision needed**: one sentence, and the date it is needed by and why.
2. **Recommendation**: the option, in one sentence, first.
3. **Options**: three real ones (doing nothing counts if it is real), in a table
   on the same criteria: outcome, cost, time, risk, and **cost of reversal** (how
   expensive it is to undo if wrong).
4. **Why this one**: the two or three facts that decide it, each marked measured
   or assumed.
5. **Risks and mitigations**: the top two for the recommended option.
6. **What approval covers**: exactly what will be done, spent or committed, and
   what will come back for another decision.

## Rules

- Prefer the option that is cheap to reverse when the evidence is thin.
- No option is a straw man; if an option is obviously bad, replace it.
- Numbers carry their source; estimates say so.
- Background goes in an appendix or a link, never above the recommendation.

## Check

Could the reader approve or reject from the first five lines? If not, rewrite the
top.
