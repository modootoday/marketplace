# Coverage and chronology audit

Users who asked an assistant for a decade of research in a field, a policy history or a genre
overview report the same defects: major items missing, dates wrong, proposals described as
rules, and real and fictional or same-name people mixed.

## Steps

1. Fix the scope and period in one line before writing. Ask if either is missing.
2. Build the list of items from at least two independent sources (for example a review
   article and a primary record) before writing prose. An item that appears in one source
   only is marked as such.
3. Give every item a date and a source, then sort by date. Mark the earliest known source,
   and say when an earlier one might exist.
4. Separate the categories that get blurred: proposed versus enacted versus in effect
   (each has its own date), claim versus finding, commentary versus policy, real versus
   fictional, and people or works that share a name.
5. Check the period for holes: a stretch with no items, an item known to be central that is
   absent, an outcome after the last item (exemption, reversal, suspension).
6. Report the coverage gaps and the searches or sources not tried, so the reader knows
   what the list does not show.

## Output

A claim-source table (date, item, status, source or "source needed"), then the gap list and
the searches not run. Do not fill a gap from memory; write it as "to search". When no search was possible, say in
one sentence that nothing in the list has been verified against a source.
