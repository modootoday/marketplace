# A month of social posts

A calendar fails when it plans more than the team can make, or when every post is
about something different. Plan small, repeat what works.

## 1. Inputs

Ask for, or state your assumption about:

- What the business sells and to whom, and the one thing to sell more of this month.
- Hours per week someone can spend making posts.
- Channels already in use, and which one brings customers.
- Dates that matter this month (launches, holidays, seasonal demand, payday).

## 2. Pillars

Three or four recurring themes, each tied to a reason to buy. For a bakery:
behind the oven (trust), today's batch (urgency), regulars and reviews (proof),
one tip a week (save-worthy). Give each pillar a fixed weekday so the plan writes
itself next month.

## 3. Cadence

Fit posts to the hours available, not to an ideal. As a starting point: about one
hour per photo post, two per short video. Three good posts a week beat seven rushed
ones. Put the most effort into the channel that brings customers.

## 4. The calendar

A table, one row per post:

| Date | Channel | Pillar | Format | Hook (first line or first second) | CTA |

- Make one idea do several jobs: a short video, a carousel of its frames, a story
  poll, and a blog post that collects them.
- Put the month's selling goal in at least one post per week, with a clear next step
  (order link, store hours, reservation).
- Mark dated posts that cannot move; the rest can swap when something timely happens.
- For Korean audiences and Naver, see `references/korea.ko.md`.

## 5. Weekly check

Each week note saves, shares, profile visits and messages or orders per post, not
likes alone. Keep the formats and hooks that led to messages or orders, drop a pillar
that gets nothing after three weeks.

## Output

The assumptions, the pillars with weekdays, the cadence, the dated calendar table,
and the three numbers to record each week.
