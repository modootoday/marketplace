# Launch announcement email

People skim a launch email for one answer: what changed for me. Lead with that.

## 1. The one message

Write one sentence: who it is for, what is new, what they can now do. Everything in
the email supports that sentence or is cut.

## 2. Subject and preview

- **Subject**: the new thing and its benefit, concrete and under about 40 characters.
  "Scheduled posts are here" beats "Big news from us".
- **Preview text**: the second half of the pitch, never "View in browser".
- Draft two or three subjects to test on a small slice before the full send.

## 3. Body

1. One line that names the change.
2. Show it: a screenshot, a 10-second GIF, a photo of the new item or the shop front.
3. Two or three short benefits, each a result the reader gets, not a feature list.
4. Proof if you have it (a beta user's line, a number with its source).
5. One call to action button that says what happens ("Try scheduled posts",
   "See the new menu"). Repeat the same link once in text, no second competing ask.
6. For an opening or a limited offer: date, time, place or code, and when it ends.

## 4. Segments

- **Existing customers**: what changes for them and anything they must do.
- **Prospects or past visitors**: the benefit first, a reason to come back.
- Do not send the same email to both if the first line would be wrong for one of them.

## 5. Before sending

- Marketing email needs the recipient's prior consent, the sender's name and contact,
  and an unsubscribe that works in one step. For Korean recipients see
  `references/send.ko.md`.
- Every link opens the right page; images have alt text; it reads on a phone and in
  dark mode; a test send is read end to end.
- Send time: for consumers, test a weekday mid-morning against an evening send; for
  business readers, a weekday morning.

## Output

The one-message sentence, two or three subject and preview pairs, the email body,
segment variants if they differ, and the pre-send checklist with anything still missing.
